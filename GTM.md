# The Game Plan (GTM)

I’ve been thinking about how to actually get people to use this. You can build the coolest auditor in the world, but if nobody knows it exists, it’s just a side project. Here’s how I’d actually take SpendShift from "cool demo" to a real lead-gen engine for Credex.

### The Target User (The "Stuck" Engineering Manager)

The person I'm building this for isn't just a "startup founder." It’s much more specific. It’s an **Engineering Manager at a Seed or Series A SaaS company with a team of 8 to 20 people.**

Why them? Because at that size, they’ve just started delegating tool choices. Half the team is on Cursor because they saw a cool tweet, the other half is still on Copilot because that’s what they used at their last job. The EM is the one who gets the $1,200 bill every month and thinks, "Wait, why is this so high?" but they have 50 other priorities (like shipping features) so they never actually sit down to audit it.

**What they're Googling before finding us:**
- "Claude Team plan vs Pro comparison"
- "How to cancel Cursor Business subscription"
- "Cursor API vs Pro for teams"
- "GitHub Copilot Enterprise minimum seats"

They aren't looking for "SaaS management software"—that sounds like a boring enterprise tool. They’re looking for a quick answer to a specific pricing question. That's where we catch them.

### Distribution Channels (How we get in front of them)

#### 1. The "Benchmark Leak" (Cold Outreach)
Instead of a generic "Save money" email, I’d send a highly specific one-liner to CTOs on LinkedIn: *"Hey, we just benchmarked 40 teams in the YC W26 batch. The average AI spend per dev is $142/mo. Based on your team size, you’re likely overpaying by ~$450. Run the 2-minute auditor here to see where the leak is."* It’s the "leak" part that gets people. Nobody wants to be the one person in their peer group who's getting ripped off.

#### 2. Community Infiltration (Not Link Spamming)
I’m targeting specific places where smart EMs actually hang out:
- **r/ExperiencedDevs**: People there love talking about "SaaS tax." I’d post a "I audited our $2k/mo AI bill and found $600 in waste" write-up. Not a link drop, but a story with a link at the bottom.
- **Rands Leadership Slack**: This is where all the real EMs are. I’d post it in #tooling as a utility tool I built to help my own team.
- **YC Bookface**: Specifically for founders who are just now scaling their stacks.

#### 3. My "Unfair" Channel: The Credex Network
This is the big one. Credex already has a bunch of companies buying credits from them. Those companies trust Credex. If Credex sends a monthly newsletter saying, "Hey, we built this free tool to help you optimize your AI spend before you buy more credits from us," the conversion rate will be insane. It positions Credex as a partner, not just a vendor. Most vendors want you to spend more; Credex is telling you how to spend *better*.

### The First 100 Users (The 30-Day Sprint)

I’m doing this with a $0 budget. It’s all sweat equity.

- **Days 1-10 (The Direct 50):** I’m going to manually find 50 CTOs at seed-stage companies on LinkedIn. I’ll send them a personalized 30-second Loom showing their company's potential "Savings Benchmark." Goal: Get the first 15 audits completed to validate the logic.
- **Days 11-20 (The Content Wedge):** I’ll write a blog post called "The Hidden Cost of the 'Team' Plan" using real data (anonymized) from the first 15 audits. I'll post this on Hacker News and Reddit. Goal: 50 audits.
- **Days 21-30 (The Referral Loop):** I’ll add a small "Compare with a Friend" button on the results page. If an EM sees they saved $400, they’ll want to show it to their founder buddy at another company. Goal: 35 audits.

### Week-1 Traction (Real World Numbers)

If this were live right now, these are the numbers I’d be looking for:
- **Audits Completed:** 25 (This is the "aha" moment).
- **Email Capture Rate:** 15%. If fewer than 1 in 6 people drop their email after seeing their savings, the value prop isn't strong enough.
- **Viral Coefficient:** 0.2. Every 5 users should result in 1 new user from a shared link.
- **The "North Star":** **Total Identified Savings.** I don't care about page views. I care about the fact that SpendShift found $4,500 in wasted spend in week one. That’s the number that proves this tool matters.
